# Scoring and attempt tracking

## What counts

- Attempt: a substantive learner answer to the current question or a submitted coding solution.
- Hint: a direct clue, worked sub-step, clarification needed to continue, or relevant example supplied before the answer is complete.
- Review: a later re-attempt of a previously graded item. Preserve the original result and add a dated review result.

Do not count small wording questions, requests to restate the prompt, or correction of a transcription mistake before an answer as attempts.

## 0–10 mastery scale

| Score | Evidence |
|---:|---|
| 10 | Fully correct and precise on first substantive attempt; no hints. |
| 9 | Correct on first attempt with only a minor terminology or precision correction. |
| 8 | Mostly correct; one meaningful detail, condition, or implication is missing. |
| 6–7 | Solid partial model, but needs a directed correction or hint on an important point. |
| 3–5 | Some relevant knowledge, but important mechanics or conclusions are missing or incorrect; multiple hints needed. |
| 1–2 | Fundamentally incorrect model or unsuccessful attempt after substantial help. |
| 0 | No demonstrated answer after choosing to skip or abandon the item. |

Use the lower end of a range when the learner answer would produce incorrect code or an unsafe conclusion. Do not reduce a score for grammar, voice transcription, or nonessential terminology.

## Coding-exercise scoring

Score behavior and explanation together:

- Correct, tested code that the learner can explain earns 9–10.
- Correct code with a weak explanation, or a small bug after a sound approach, is typically 7–8.
- A partial scaffold or approach with major missing logic is typically 3–6.
- Code copied without demonstrated understanding needs a transfer question before a high score.
